# Content

The words are part of the design. Most designs that read as generated read that
way because of the copy, not the layout.

## Never invent facts

No invented customers, logos, testimonials, star ratings, adoption numbers,
prices, dates or quotes. If the brief did not supply a number, the design does
not get one. A section with nothing true to say is a section to delete, not a
section to fill.

Placeholder content is allowed only when it is visibly placeholder: a labelled
box, a monospace note. Lorem ipsum hides every layout problem until it is
expensive to fix.

## The user's words are the user's

Text handed to you goes in verbatim. Design it — set it, break it, emphasise it
— but do not rewrite it, tighten it, or "improve" it. If it genuinely does not
fit, say so and ask.

## When you write the copy

Write plainly, in the register the audience uses. Say the specific thing:
"Renders a 12-slide deck to PDF in four seconds" carries more than "Powerful
export capabilities".

Avoid the tells:

- "It's not X — it's Y" constructions, and their cousins.
- Metadiscourse: "here's why this matters", "let's dive in", "the key insight".
- Emphasis on every third phrase. If everything is important, nothing is.
- Em-dash pile-ups and one-sentence paragraphs used for drama.
- Filler abstractions: seamless, elevate, empower, unlock, leverage, robust.
- Rhetorical questions as headings.

Convey what you were given. Do not editorialise about implications.

## Length follows the medium

- Slide: one idea. A title plus at most three lines, or a title plus one image,
  or a title plus one number. Speaker notes carry the rest.
- Landing page: one promise, one reason to believe it, one action.
- Document: whatever the argument needs, in an order the reader can follow.
- Email: the point in the first screenful; everything else is optional reading.

## Structure the argument first

Decide the story before the layout: what the reader knows at the start, what
they should know at the end, and the shortest path between them. A deck whose
slide titles read as a coherent list in order is already most of the way there;
one that needs the slides to make sense is not finished.

## Labels and microcopy

Buttons say what happens ("Start the export"), not what they are ("Submit").
Empty states say what to do next. Error text says what went wrong and what fixes
it. The same action keeps the same label everywhere in one artifact.

## Language

Write in the language the brief is in, and keep it consistent across the whole
artifact — including button labels, dates and units. A page that mixes two
languages reads as unfinished.
