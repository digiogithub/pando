# Anti-slop

Generated designs converge on the same page. These are the tells, and what to do instead.

## Banned by default

- **The purple-to-blue gradient hero.** Also its cousins: gradient text, gradient borders,
  gradient buttons. Use one flat colour and earn attention with type and space.
- **Emoji as iconography.** 🚀 in a headline, ✨ in a feature list, ✅ in a checklist. Use real
  icons or no icons.
- **Glassmorphism everywhere** — `backdrop-filter: blur()` on every card, translucent panels
  stacked on translucent panels. It costs contrast and buys nothing.
- **Three feature cards in a row, each with an icon, a two-word heading and one sentence of
  filler.** If the three things are not genuinely parallel, do not put them in a row.
- **Filler copy that says nothing**: "Seamlessly elevate your workflow", "Empower your team
  to unlock potential". Write what the thing does or leave the space empty.
- **Fake social proof**: invented logos, invented testimonials, invented star ratings,
  "Trusted by 10,000+ teams". Never fabricate a number, a quote, or a customer.
- **Centred everything.** Centred text over ~2 lines is hard to read; the ragged left edge
  costs the reader a re-scan on every line.
- **Decorative floating blobs and abstract SVG shapes** that carry no meaning.
- **Card shadows on every element.** Elevation means something; universal elevation means
  nothing.

## Do instead

- Say the specific thing. "Renders a 12-slide deck to PDF in 4 seconds" beats "Powerful
  export capabilities".
- Let one element be loud and keep the rest quiet.
- Use real content early. Lorem ipsum hides every layout problem until it is expensive.
- If a section exists only because pages usually have one, delete the section.

## Self-check before finishing

1. Would a designer recognise this as a template? Change what makes it recognisable.
2. Is any claim on the page invented? Remove it.
3. Does every colour, size and gap come from the design system?
4. Does it survive a 375px viewport and a 4.5:1 contrast check?
